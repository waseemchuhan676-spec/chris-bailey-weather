How to Interpret Weather Radar

Understand Doppler reflectivity, storm motion and severe weather warnings.

⚡

Base Reflectivity (dBZ)

Reflectivity measures the amount of transmitted energy reflected back by precipitation. Values above 50 dBZ indicate intense rainfall and potential hail cores.

🌪️

Doppler Velocity

Doppler velocity detects wind direction toward or away from the radar dish, helping meteorologists detect rotation, microbursts and tornado signatures.

❄️

Dual-Polarization

Dual-pol radar transmits both horizontal and vertical pulses to distinguish between rain, snow, hail, and airborne tornado debris (Tornadic Debris Signatures).

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about Chris Bailey weather radar and storm tracking.

How do I track live storms on Chris Bailey Weather Radar?

Use the station preset buttons above the radar to select local Kentucky Doppler stations (Central Kentucky KLVX, Eastern Kentucky KJKL, Western Kentucky KPAH) or switch to the national radar mosaic.

What do the colors mean on Doppler weather radar?

Green indicates light to moderate rain, yellow and orange indicate heavy downpours, red and magenta indicate intense thunderstorms or hail, and blue indicates snow.

How often does National Weather Service radar update?

NEXRAD Doppler radars complete a full volume scan every 4 to 10 minutes depending on whether precipitation mode or clear-air scanning is currently active.

What is the difference between a severe watch and warning?

A watch means conditions are favorable for severe storms or tornadoes to develop. A warning means dangerous weather has been detected by radar or spotters and immediate shelter is required.
