How to Read a Forecast

Use current conditions, hourly details and the extended outlook to make practical plans.

How to Check Today's Conditions

A useful local forecast starts with the conditions that matter right now. The current temperature tells you how warm or cold the air is, while the feels-like value can better reflect how wind and humidity affect comfort. Humidity may make warm conditions feel heavier, and wind can make cold conditions feel more severe.

Also check current conditions and the probability of precipitation. A rain chance is useful for deciding whether to carry an umbrella, adjust travel plans or move outdoor activities. Wind direction and speed can matter for commuting, boating, aviation and severe-storm awareness.

How to Read a 7-Day Outlook

The 7-day local forecast gives a broader view of changing conditions. Compare high and low temperatures, precipitation probability and daily conditions instead of focusing on a single number. Forecast periods can change as new observations and forecast models become available.

For outdoor plans, look for the most comfortable temperature window, check the chance of rain or snow, and review wind conditions. The extended forecast is best used for planning trends rather than treating every detail several days ahead as fixed.

Forecast by ZIP Code

Use the search box to enter a U.S. ZIP code, city and state, such as 75201, Dallas, TX or Charlotte, NC. The site uses location search to find the selected place and then requests the appropriate National Weather Service forecast data for that area.

This makes it easier to move from national USA forecast information to a local local forecast when you need details for where you live, work or travel.

How to Use Radar

Live radar is useful for following precipitation and storm movement. Radar displays where rain, snow or other precipitation is being detected and can help you see whether activity is moving toward or away from your area.

Radar colors generally represent different precipitation intensity, but the exact interpretation depends on the radar product. Use the radar together with official severe weather alerts because radar alone does not replace watches, warnings or emergency instructions.

Severe Weather Safety Guides

Use official emergency guidance when dangerous conditions threaten.

⛈️ Severe Storms & Tornadoes

A weather watch means conditions are favorable for dangerous weather. A warning means hazardous weather is occurring, imminent or detected and action may be needed. An advisory generally communicates less severe but still potentially hazardous conditions.

  • Keep emergency alerts enabled.
  • Have a reliable way to receive warnings.
  • Move to appropriate shelter when instructed.
  • Follow local emergency-management instructions.

🌡️ Heat & Winter Conditions

Extreme heat, winter storms, lightning, flooding and hurricanes can become dangerous quickly. Check the local forecast before outdoor activities, avoid floodwaters, seek shelter from lightning, prepare for rapid temperature changes and follow evacuation or emergency instructions from official authorities.

  • Plan around dangerous heat or cold.
  • Check vulnerable family members and neighbors.
  • Do not drive through flooded roads.
  • Follow official National Weather Service guidance.
